#### McClintock Letters: One Year Later

Thank you again to those of you who supported the McClintock Letters initiative last year. For those of you who may not know, this initiative was a main coalescing force for SNAP itself, and we ended up enabling the publication of over 200 pieces in local newspapers across the country in which scientists communicated their work to their neighbors and hometowns, reaching tens of thousands of people who may not have otherwise been exposed to cutting-edge research and its importance.

The strides we made in public awareness of ongoing research and community-building among scientists and the public cannot be overstated, earning us the Research!America Meeting the Moment for Public Health Award and dozens of national and international [press appearances](https://snapcoalition.org/initiatives/mcclintock-letters/), and we are so grateful for everyone who spread the word and participated. The success of this project, fueled by collective participation across the scientific community, has afforded SNAP access to platforms and spaces that we may not otherwise have been invited to as such a young organization, and we strive to use our influence for the good of the community.

Several SNAPpers have spearheaded a new science journalism project in partnership with local newspapers developing a model for community-oriented science communication to carry on the mission of the McClintock Letters. Visit the [Innovations in Science website](https://innovationsinscience.com/) to learn more, support, or get involved!

#### Friends and Family for Scientists (Mini) Initiative: Responding to the OMB's Proposed Federal Financial Assistance Rule

Invite community stakeholders, friends, and family to respond to the changes to federal funding proposed by the Office of Management and Budget (Docket OMB-2026-0034). The proposed rule would lead to substantive changes in federal funding for science along with many other areas of public impact. Following SNAP's webinar, "Crafting a Public Comment: Understanding the Proposed OMB Rule," with Allie Cashel from [Protect Democracy](https://protectdemocracy.org/), we wanted to a) provide an overview of the proposed changes for those outside of the science community and b) offer a resource for writing public comments on this rule.

Access the one pager [here](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fo/qds1bdej018pk2073ulz3/ABbsNVbR91CjGy9VZNnXknw?rlkey=0x3tsss1pvc3b3bcc16rf5mgk&st=epmd3557&dl=0) to share with concerned community members or others who may be impacted by the proposed rules! While these were created for a broad audience, if you have any contacts for non-scientist stakeholders (e.g., patient advocacy groups) who would benefit from a more tailored resource, reach out to [snapscipolorg@gmail.com](mailto:snapscipolorg@gmail.com) with the subject line, "OMB Proposed Rule Stakeholder Contact" to discuss a more targeted one-pager.

        - A 24% decrease in project grant funding from science agencies between fiscal 2024 and 2025.
        - A loss of more than 117,000 employees working in federal science agencies between September 2024 and February 2026, accounting for 41% of the total federal workforce reduction during that period.
